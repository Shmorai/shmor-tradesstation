Garden rooms, sheds and decking: the rules

Garden offices, summerhouses, sheds and decks can often be built without a planning application. Here are the size and height limits, and when Building Regulations come into it.

By the TradesStation editorial team · 14 October 2026 · 2 min read

Garden rooms and outbuildings: planning limits

  • Single storey only, with eaves no higher than 2.5 metres.
  • Maximum overall height of 4 metres with a dual-pitched roof, or 3 metres for any other roof.
  • Within 2 metres of a boundary, the whole building must be no higher than 2.5 metres.
  • Not on land in front of the principal elevation of the house.
  • Together with extensions and other buildings, no more than half the land around the original house.
  • No verandas, balconies or raised platforms.

The building must be for a use incidental to the house, such as a home office, gym or hobby room. Separate self-contained living accommodation is not covered and needs planning permission.

Extra limits in some areas

  • Within the grounds of a listed building, any outbuilding needs planning permission.
  • On designated land (such as conservation areas), outbuildings at the side of the house need permission.
  • In National Parks, the Broads, Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty and World Heritage Sites, outbuildings more than 20 metres from the house are limited to 10 square metres in total.

Decking

  • Decking or other platforms can be permitted development if no more than 30cm above the ground.
  • Decking, outbuildings and extensions together must cover no more than half the garden.
  • Not in front of the principal elevation, and not within the grounds of a listed building.

Higher, raised decks need planning permission.

Building Regulations

  • A detached outbuilding under 15 square metres with no sleeping accommodation does not normally need Building Regulations approval.
  • Between 15 and 30 square metres, approval is normally not needed if there is no sleeping accommodation and the building is at least 1 metre from any boundary, or is built substantially of non-combustible materials.
  • Larger buildings, or any with sleeping accommodation, normally need approval.
  • Electrical work in a garden room, such as a new circuit run from the house, is notifiable. Use a registered electrician.

Before you build

Check with your council if you are unsure, especially if your home is listed, a flat, or in a protected area. A lawful development certificate can confirm that a project is permitted development. For groundwork, bases and decking, compare local landscapers; for timber structures, see carpenters and joiners.

Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own planning rules. This guide covers England; elsewhere in the UK, check with your local council.

Last updated: 14 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

Do I need planning permission for a garden room?
Often not, if it meets the permitted development limits: single storey, within the height limits (2.5 metres within 2 metres of a boundary), behind the front of the house and covering no more than half the garden with other buildings.
Can I sleep in my garden room?
Using an outbuilding as a bedroom or separate living accommodation is not covered by permitted development and brings in Building Regulations. Check with your council first.
How high can decking be without planning permission?
Up to 30cm above the ground, provided it and other buildings cover no more than half the garden, and it is not at the front of the house or at a listed building.
