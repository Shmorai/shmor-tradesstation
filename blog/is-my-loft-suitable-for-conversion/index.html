Is my loft suitable for a conversion? 7 checks

Before you get quotes, it helps to know whether your loft has the basics for a conversion. These are the things a loft specialist or architect will look at first.

By the TradesStation editorial team · 14 October 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Marketing Department on Unsplash

On this page

  2. 2. Roof structure
  3. 3. Room for stairs
  4. 4. Walls and foundations
  5. 5. Services in the loft
  6. 6. Neighbours
  7. 7. Planning and protected areas

1. Head height

Measure from the top of the ceiling joists to the underside of the ridge at the highest point. Many loft specialists use around 2.2 metres as a rule of thumb for a straightforward conversion; this is a practical guide, not a regulation. Lower lofts may still work with a dormer, a mansard, or by lowering ceilings below, but that adds cost.

2. Roof structure

Older homes often have traditional rafters and purlins, which leave open space. Many homes built from the 1960s onwards have trussed rafters (W-shaped timbers), which need more structural work to remove and replace with steel or new timbers. Both can usually be converted with the right design.

3. Room for stairs

A habitable loft needs a fixed staircase, with suitable headroom above it. Work out where it could land on the floor below, and what space you would lose. See staircase rules.

4. Walls and foundations

The new floor and any steel beams usually bear on existing walls. An engineer will check that the walls and foundations can take the extra load.

5. Services in the loft

Water tanks, flues, chimney breasts and wiring may need moving or removing. Removing a chimney breast is structural work that needs proper support and sign-off.

6. Neighbours

In semi-detached and terraced homes, steel beams often go into the party wall, which usually means a party wall notice.

7. Planning and protected areas

Many loft conversions are permitted development, but not in conservation areas, for flats or listed buildings. See loft conversion planning rules and loft conversion types.

When you are ready, compare loft conversion specialists.

Frequently asked questions

What head height do I need for a loft conversion?
There is no single legal minimum for the loft itself, but many specialists use around 2.2 metres (from the top of the ceiling joists to the underside of the ridge) as a rule of thumb for a straightforward conversion.
Can a loft with trussed rafters be converted?
Usually yes, but it needs more structural work, often with steel beams, to replace the trusses. An engineer will design the new structure.
