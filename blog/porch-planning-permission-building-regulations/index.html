Porch planning permission: the 3m² rule and what else to check

Adding a porch is one of the simplest home improvements, and in England it often needs no planning application at all. But it only works if the porch stays inside three size and position limits, and your house has to be the right type. Here is how to check your own plan before you ask a builder to quote.

Do you need planning permission for a porch?

Usually not. The Planning Portal explains that adding a porch to any external door of a house in England is permitted development, so no planning application is needed, as long as all three of these are true:

  • The ground floor area of the porch, measured externally, does not exceed 3 square metres.
  • No part of it is more than 3 metres above ground level.
  • No part of it is within 2 metres of any boundary that fronts a highway (a road, pavement or footpath).

If your plan breaks even one of those limits, you need to apply for planning permission. The rules above are for England; the Planning Portal notes that the policy in Wales may differ, so check with your own council if you are elsewhere.

When the permitted development rights do not apply

The limits above apply to houses. The Planning Portal says they do not cover:

  • Flats and maisonettes.
  • Converted houses, or houses created through certain permitted development rights such as changes of use or new dwellinghouses.
  • Other buildings.
  • Homes where a planning condition, Article 4 Direction or other restriction removes permitted development rights.

Newer estates often carry such conditions, and listed buildings and conservation areas have their own extra controls, so ask your local planning authority before you commit. Many councils offer a certificate of lawful development if you want written confirmation that a porch is permitted development. See our wider guide to extension planning permission rules for the same checks on larger projects.

Does a porch need Building Regulations approval?

Planning and Building Regulations are separate. The Planning Portal says that a porch built at ground level and under 30 square metres in floor area is normally exempt from Building Regulations approval, provided that:

  • the glazing and any fixed electrical installations comply with the relevant sections of the regulations;
  • the front door between the house and the porch stays in place; and
  • if the house has ramped or level access for disabled people, the porch does not make that access worse.

Note that the 3 square metre planning limit and the 30 square metre Building Regulations limit are different numbers for different purposes. If you remove the inner door to make the porch part of your hall, it becomes a more complicated extension; read Building Regulations for extensions and lofts first.

Glazing, lights and sockets: the parts people forget

Because the exemption depends on glazing and electrics complying, these two details matter most. Glass in doors and low windows is a safety-sensitive area, so ask your builder or glazier to confirm the glass they supply meets the regulations. Any new lighting, outdoor sockets or a doorbell supply should be installed by a competent electrician; see our electricians guide for what to check before you hire.

Do not assume that a design you saw on a neighbour's house is allowed on yours: it may have been built under different rules, a different planning decision, or without permission.

Questions to ask before you hire

A porch is a small job, but small jobs still go wrong when the details are vague. Before you accept a quote, ask each builder:

  • What external floor area and height have they priced, and have they checked it against the 3 square metre and 3 metre limits?
  • Who is responsible for checking whether permitted development rights have been removed on your property?
  • How will the new porch roof and walls tie in to the existing house, and how will they deal with rainwater and damp?
  • Which glazing and electrical work is included, and who will carry out and certify the electrics?
  • What is the payment schedule, and what happens if the design has to change?

Written answers make it far easier to compare quotes like for like. If a builder tells you that no rules apply to a porch, treat that as a prompt to double-check with your council.

Planning your porch and finding a builder

Before you ask for quotes, measure the proposed footprint on the outside of the walls and mark the 2 metre line from any boundary that faces a road. Then write down the finished height at the highest point. Having those three numbers lets every builder quote for the same job and shows you quickly whether you are inside the limits.

Frequently asked questions

What size porch can I build without planning permission?
In England, a porch to an external door of a house is permitted development if its ground floor area is no more than 3 square metres measured externally, no part is over 3 metres high, and no part is within 2 metres of a boundary that fronts a highway (Planning Portal).
Can I add a porch to a flat?
The permitted development rights for porches do not apply to flats and maisonettes, so you would normally need planning permission, and your lease may also restrict external changes.
Do I need Building Regulations approval for a porch?
Normally not, if it is at ground level and under 30 square metres, the glazing and fixed electrics comply, the door between the house and porch stays in place, and disabled access is not made worse (Planning Portal).
What if my house is in a conservation area?
Extra restrictions can apply in conservation areas and on listed buildings, and some homes have permitted development rights removed. Ask your local planning authority before you start.
