Problems with your builder? Your rights and options

Most building jobs go well, but if something goes wrong, there is a clear path to follow. Here is how to raise a complaint, what the law says and where to go for help.

By the TradesStation editorial team · 14 October 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Amina Atar on Unsplash

On this page

  1. Your rights
  2. What you can ask for
  3. Steps to take
  4. Going to court
  5. Avoiding problems next time

Your rights

  • Under the Consumer Rights Act 2015, a trader must carry out a service with reasonable care and skill.
  • If no price or time was agreed, they must charge a reasonable price and finish within a reasonable time.
  • What the builder told you, and you relied on, can form part of the contract.

What you can ask for

If the work is not up to standard, you can ask the builder to put it right (repeat the work) within a reasonable time, at their cost and without significant inconvenience to you. If that is impossible, or they do not do it in a reasonable time, you can ask for a price reduction, which in some cases can be the full price.

Steps to take

  • Keep records: the contract, quotes, invoices, messages and dated photos.
  • Raise it early and in writing, explaining what is wrong and what you want done, with a reasonable deadline.
  • Withhold only what is fair: it can be reasonable to hold back a proportionate amount while defects are fixed, but take advice before withholding large sums.
  • Use a scheme: if the builder belongs to a trade body or TrustMark, use its complaints or dispute resolution process.
  • Get advice: Citizens Advice can explain your options, and pass details to Trading Standards.

Going to court

If nothing else works, you can make a claim for money through the county court in England and Wales; claims for smaller amounts are usually dealt with on the small claims track. Mediation can be quicker and cheaper, and the court will expect you to have tried to resolve things first. Scotland and Northern Ireland have different processes.

Avoiding problems next time

Most disputes can be avoided with a clear written contract, stage payments, and proper checks before you hire. See our guide to choosing a builder.

Last updated: 14 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

What can I do if my builder’s work is poor?
Ask them in writing to put it right within a reasonable time. Under the Consumer Rights Act, if they cannot or do not, you can ask for a price reduction. Use a trade body scheme if they belong to one, and get advice from Citizens Advice.
Can I take my builder to court?
Yes, as a last resort. In England and Wales you can make a money claim through the county court, often on the small claims track. Try to resolve the dispute or use mediation first.
