Fence, wall and gate height rules

Most garden fences, walls and gates do not need planning permission, as long as they stay under set heights. Here are the limits, the exceptions and what to agree with your neighbours.

By the TradesStation editorial team · 14 October 2026 · 2 min read

Photo: Daniel Janzen on Unsplash

On this page

  1. The height limits
  2. When you need permission
  3. Hedges
  4. Boundaries and neighbours
  5. Find a fencing contractor

The height limits

In England, you generally do not need planning permission to put up, alter or replace a fence, wall or gate if:

  • it is no more than 1 metre high where it is next to a road used by vehicles (or the footpath of such a road); and
  • it is no more than 2 metres high anywhere else.

If an existing fence or wall is already over these heights, you cannot make it taller without permission.

When you need permission

  • Your home is a listed building, or the fence is within its grounds.
  • The boundary is shared with a neighbouring listed building or its grounds.
  • Your permitted development rights have been removed by a planning condition or Article 4 direction (common on some open-plan estates).
  • In a conservation area, you may need permission to remove a fence, wall or gate.

Hedges

Planting a hedge does not usually need planning permission, although some estates have planning conditions or covenants that restrict planting at the front. If a neighbour’s evergreen hedge is causing a problem, try to resolve it with them first; councils can deal with some high hedge complaints as a last resort.

Boundaries and neighbours

  • Check your title deeds or plans to see who owns and maintains each boundary.
  • Talk to your neighbour before replacing a shared fence.
  • Building a garden wall on the boundary line may fall under the Party Wall etc. Act.
  • Tall freestanding brick walls need proper foundations and design to be stable. An experienced builder or landscaper will advise.

Find a fencing contractor

Ask about post type and depth, how the posts will be fixed, gravel boards to keep panels off the ground, and the treatment and guarantee for the timber. Compare local landscapers and fencing contractors.

Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own planning rules. This guide covers England; elsewhere in the UK, check with your local council.

Frequently asked questions

How high can my back garden fence be without planning permission?
Up to 2 metres, as long as it is not next to a road used by vehicles and your home is not listed or subject to an Article 4 direction.
How high can a front garden fence or wall be?
Up to 1 metre where it is next to a road used by vehicles or its footpath. Higher than that normally needs planning permission.
Does trellis on top of a fence count towards the height?
Generally yes, the overall height is what counts. If adding trellis takes the total above the limit, check with your council.
