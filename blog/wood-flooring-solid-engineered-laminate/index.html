Solid wood, engineered wood or laminate flooring?

Wood-effect floors come in three main types that look similar but behave very differently. Here is how to choose the right one for each room.

By the TradesStation editorial team · 14 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Denis GeoModule on Unsplash

On this page

  1. Solid wood
  2. Engineered wood
  3. Laminate
  4. Fitting matters
  5. Find a fitter

Solid wood

  • Each board is a single piece of timber.
  • Can be sanded and refinished many times over its life.
  • Moves with changes in humidity and temperature, so it is less suited to kitchens, bathrooms, conservatories and many underfloor heating systems.
  • Often nailed or glued to a suitable subfloor.

Engineered wood

  • A real wood top layer bonded to layers of plywood or softwood beneath.
  • More stable than solid wood, so it is often recommended over underfloor heating and in rooms with more variation in temperature (check the manufacturer’s guidance).
  • Can be sanded, but how many times depends on the thickness of the top layer.

Laminate

  • A printed image of wood under a hard-wearing clear layer, on a fibreboard core.
  • Usually the most budget-friendly, and resistant to scratches.
  • Cannot be sanded. Standard laminate does not like standing water; water-resistant versions exist.

Fitting matters

  • Subfloor: must be flat, dry and sound. New screeds need to be dry before wood goes down.
  • Acclimatisation: many products need to sit in the room before fitting; follow the manufacturer’s instructions.
  • Expansion gaps: wood floors need gaps at the edges, hidden under skirting or beading.
  • Underlay: the right underlay helps with sound, warmth and moisture. In flats, check your lease for any sound insulation requirements before laying hard floors.

Find a fitter

Ask your fitter what is included: removing old flooring, preparing the subfloor, underlay, door bars, and refitting or replacing skirting. Compare local carpenters and flooring fitters.

Frequently asked questions

Which wood flooring is best with underfloor heating?
Engineered wood is usually the better choice, as it is more stable than solid wood. Always check the manufacturer confirms the product is suitable for your heating system.
Can I put wood flooring in a kitchen?
Engineered wood and water-resistant laminate are often used in kitchens. Solid wood is more sensitive to moisture and spills, so it needs more care.
Can I lay laminate in my flat?
Check your lease first. Many leases require carpets or specific sound-insulating underlay to reduce noise for neighbours below.
