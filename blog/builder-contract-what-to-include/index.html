Builder contracts: what to include before work starts

A clear written contract is the best protection for you and your builder. It sets expectations, reduces arguments and gives you something to point to if things go wrong.

By the TradesStation editorial team · 14 October 2026 · 2 min read

On this page

  1. Why put it in writing?
  2. What the contract should cover
  3. Ready-made contracts
  4. Your rights still apply

Why put it in writing?

Most building disputes come from different expectations about what was agreed. A written contract records the job, the price and the rules for changes, so both sides know where they stand. For anything more than a small job, do not rely on a verbal agreement or a one-line quote.

What the contract should cover

  • The parties: your name and the builder’s full business name and address.
  • Scope of work: refer to the drawings and a written specification, including materials and finishes.
  • Price: whether it is fixed or an estimate, whether VAT is included, and what is excluded (for example skips, making good, decorating).
  • Payment stages: amounts linked to completed stages of work, with a sum held back until the end.
  • Dates: start date, expected finish date and what happens if the work runs late.
  • Changes (variations): how extra work or changes will be priced and agreed in writing before they happen.
  • Permissions: who is responsible for planning, Building Regulations, party wall notices and certificates.
  • Site rules: working hours, access, parking, toilet use, waste removal and tidying up.
  • Insurance: the builder’s public liability and, for bigger jobs, contract works insurance.
  • Guarantees and defects: how snagging and defects after completion will be dealt with.
  • Disputes: how disagreements will be resolved, such as through a trade body or adjudication.

Ready-made contracts

You do not have to write a contract from scratch. The Joint Contracts Tribunal (JCT) publishes Home Owner contracts designed for homeowners in England and Wales hiring a builder for small domestic works such as extensions and alterations, with versions for when you have, or have not, appointed a consultant such as an architect. Some trade bodies also provide contracts for their members’ customers.

Your rights still apply

Whatever the contract says, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 means a trader must carry out the work with reasonable care and skill. If the contract is agreed in your home rather than at the builder’s premises, you usually have a 14-day right to cancel, and the builder must tell you about it in writing.

Before you sign

  • Read every page, including the small print.
  • Make sure the drawings and specification are attached or referenced.
  • Check the payment stages match the work, and avoid large upfront payments.
  • Keep a signed copy.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a written contract with my builder?
It is not a legal requirement, but for anything more than a small job a written contract is strongly recommended. It protects both you and the builder.
What is a JCT Home Owner contract?
A standard building contract published by the Joint Contracts Tribunal for homeowners in England and Wales carrying out small domestic works, such as extensions and alterations.
